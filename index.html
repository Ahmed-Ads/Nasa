    How to Play

    1. Read the forecast. Space is full of surprises — the forecast tells you what is coming this sol.
    2. Spend your 6 power cells. Tap + to send cells to Oxygen, Shielding or Food. You cannot spend more than 6.
    3. Run the sol. The outpost powers up, the hazard hits, and your meters change.
    4. Keep every meter above 20%. Drop below and the crew loses a heart. Lose all 3 hearts — or let any meter reach 0 — and the mission ends.

    You get one free Revive per mission. Keyboard: 1 2 3 add a cell to O₂ / Shield / Food, Shift+number removes one, Space runs the sol.

    Mission Science

    Every number in this game comes from real space engineering — simplified so a 7-year-old can feel the trade-off.

    In-game ruleReal NASA scienceHow we simplified it
    Life support burns power ISS ECLSS recycles about 90% of water and makes oxygen from electrolysis (NASA ECLSS) 1 power cell = +8 O₂; daily wear grows as the mission gets longer
    Solar flares Solar flares and radiation storms (NASA Space Weather) Damage = 50 − 12 × shield cells, so shields matter
    Lunar night Most of the Moon gets 14 Earth-days of darkness (NASA Moon facts) Greenhouse output halves and heaters cost +10 O₂
    Cold soak LRO's Diviner: about +120 °C by day, −170 °C at night, below −200 °C in polar craters Abstracted into a "cold drain" that rises each sol
    Micrometeoroids Lunar dust and micrometeoroid research (NASA) Hits wear shielding; with zero shield power the hull leaks O₂
    Water ice LRO and LCROSS found water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters Ice events add +16 O₂ from electrolysis

    This is an educational model, not engineering-grade. Exact dataset names, URLs and formulas are listed in README.md so judges can trace every rule.
