            How to Play

            1. 1. Plan power. Spend power cells. Leftover cells charge the battery.
            2. 2. Go outside. Walk with WASD or the joystick. Hold E or ACT to do a job. Suit air drains outside. It refills each day.
            3. 3. End the day. The base makes power. The crew uses supplies. Hazards happen.
            4. Keep everyone safe. Keep meters above 20%. Zero hearts ends the mission. Retry is free. You get one Revive.
            5. Panels: press P to switch between Full and Focus. On phones use the Panels button.

            Stations: solar field (sweep dust, deploy a panel), battery bank, O₂/CO₂/water units (adjust cells on the spot), comms dish (detailed storm forecast), rover + ice patch (haul ice), bunker (shelter order), recycler (leak steps in 1.2).

            Mission Science

            Resources in Level 1

            Every rule starts from real space engineering. Values we could verify on NASA pages are marked real; everything tuned for play is marked game assumption.

            In-game rule Real NASA science Simplification
            O₂ from water ISS oxygen comes from electrolysis of water (Oxygen Generation Assembly, ECLSS) Game: 1 O₂ cell = +10 O₂ and uses 2 water
            Water recycling ISS ECLSS recovers about 98% of water (2023 NASA update) Game: 1 water cell = +10 water
            Crew use NASA life-support baselines: crews need oxygen, water and food every day Game: crew of 3 uses 10 O₂ and 6 water each day
            Leak response ISS procedure: close hatches, find the leak, Kapton tape, then epoxy. The 2018 Soyuz leak was a drilled hole — not a micrometeoroid Game: burst drains 30 water each day → isolate (40%) → tape (15%) → 2 repair cells → reopen
            Solar particle events Crews shelter in place; ~4 g/cm² of regolith gets below 30-day limits. CMEs take about 1–4 days to arrive (NASA DONKI) Game: the storm arrives on Day 3-5. Early shelter cuts dose by 90%.
            Cosmic rays Extra shielding barely reduces galactic cosmic rays Game: shielding slows dose but never zeroes it
            Long Shadow South-pole crater rims get near-constant sunlight; terrain can still shadow arrays Game: the event removes 2 solar cells for one day
            Lunar dust Lunar dust is abrasive and degrades solar panels and seals (NASA lunar dust research) Game: dust event → sweep the panels outside restores output
            Water ice Shackleton crater holds ice in permanently shadowed regions (LRO, LCROSS) Game: ice haul = +14 water

            Sources: NASA ECLSS / ISS, NASA Space Weather & DONKI, NASA Moon (LRO/Diviner/LCROSS), NASA radiation research. Full list in README.md. Educational model, not engineering-grade.
